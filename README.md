# Employee Attrition Analysis

## Overview

This project presents an exploratory data analysis (EDA) of IBM's HR Analytics – Employee Attrition & Performance dataset to identify the key factors influencing employee turnover. The analysis explores workforce trends, examines attrition patterns, and generates actionable business insights to support data-driven HR decision-making.

This project was completed as part of the AnalystLab Africa Data Science Internship Programme (Week 1: Business Understanding & Data Exploration).

## Objectives

- Understand the business problem of employee attrition.
- Inspect and assess the quality of a real-world HR dataset.
- Perform exploratory data analysis (EDA) and visualization.
- Identify the key factors associated with employee attrition.
- Generate business-focused insights to support HR decision-making.

## Dataset

IBM HR Analytics – Employee Attrition & Performance

- Records: 1,470 employees
- Features: 35 variables
- Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/pavansubhasht/ibm-hr-analytics-attrition-dataset

## Skills Demonstrated

## Key Findings

- The overall employee attrition rate is 16.1%.
- Employees who work overtime are significantly more likely to leave the organization.
- Sales Representatives have the highest attrition rate among all job roles (approximately **50%**).
- Employees who leave tend to be younger and earn lower monthly incomes than those who remain.
- Research & Development has the highest number of employee departures, while Sales records the highest attrition rate relative to its workforce size.
